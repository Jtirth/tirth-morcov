MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION

The math behind the next word

Every prediction in the application is computed from observed training-corpus counts. This page distinguishes frequency from probability and shows a worked example calculated from a separate three-sentence file.

Frequency versus probability

A frequency is a raw count. A probability is a relative frequency: a count divided by the total number of relevant observations. A count of 4 says how often an event appeared; a probability of 0.40 says it appeared in 40% of the observations considered.

Relative frequency = count of event / total observations

Conditional probability

Conditional probability asks how likely event B is after observing context A. In a next-word task, A is the context (one or two prior tokens) and B is a candidate following token.

P(B | A) = Count(A followed by B) / Count(A)

For a second-order model, the context contains two previous words:

P(C | A, B) = Count(A, B, C) / Count(A, B)

Probability distribution

For a context with observed continuations, each outcome probability is its transition count divided by the total context count. Adding the probabilities for all observed next words gives 1 (allowing for tiny display-rounding differences).

Markov property and model order

The Markov assumption says the next state is estimated using a finite recent context rather than the entire history. In this text demonstration, first order uses one previous word; second order uses the previous pair. This is a deliberate statistical simplification, not a description of how modern neural language models work.

Transition probability matrix

A transition matrix places current states in rows, possible next states in columns, and conditional transition probabilities in cells. Each row with at least one observed transition sums to 1. The dashboard lists the observed entries as tables rather than displaying a dense vocabulary-by-vocabulary matrix.

Prediction accuracy and coverage

Top-1 accuracy counts when the first-ranked prediction matches the actual next token; top-3 counts when it appears among the first three candidates. Coverage is the share of evaluable test contexts found in the training data. Accuracy is calculated over covered contexts and must be interpreted alongside coverage.
